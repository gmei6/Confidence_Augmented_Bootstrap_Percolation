\section{The Setup}
\vspace{-0.3em}
The existing baseline (Janson) gives a bank exactly \textbf{one way to fail}: enough of its neighbors have already activated.

\vspace{0.3em}
Janson et al.\ solved that case on the Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi graph, as bootstrap percolation with threshold $r$. I add a \textbf{second way for nodes to activate}.

\vspace{-1.5em}
\section{The Model}
\vspace{-0.3em}
A node is activated if either condition is met:
\vspace{0.2em}
% Converted equations to inline math ($...$) to save ~2 inches of vertical space!
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=0.3em]
\item \textbf{Percolation:} At least $r$ of its neighbors have failed ($|\{j \sim i : j \in A_t\}| \geq r$).
\item \textbf{Fear:} A Bernoulli variable with probability $f_ig_t$ that a node activates ($U_i < f_i\, g_t$).
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{-1.5em}
\section{The Comparisons}
\vspace{-0.3em}
We hold network size $n$ and average degree constant across all tests to isolate three items:
\vspace{0.2em}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=0.3em]
  \item \textbf{Heterogeneity (ER vs.\ power-law network (CM)):} Homogeneous degree distribution vs.\ heavy-tailed hubs.
  \item \textbf{Geometry (power-law network (CM) vs.\ GIRG):} Pure random connections vs.\ local spatial clustering.
  \item \textbf{Fear ($\bar\mu = 0$ vs.\ $\bar\mu > 0$):} Baseline bootstrap percolation vs.\ confidence augmentation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Fear Amplifies}
%% ⚠ DO NOT DROP THE DIAL SENTENCE (final paragraph). Without it this reads as a
%% flat "always 3x", which is the exact claim S-058 caught and retracted on the
%% advisor site: the multiplier is ~3x at moderate mu-bar but only ~1.5x at
%% mu-bar = 0.1. It is constant IN n at fixed mu-bar (gated result, power-law/CM)
%% -- not constant ACROSS mu-bar. See okf/next-actions.md, "Multiplier claim
%% qualified".
%% Famcompare provenance (2026-08-04): numbers from
%% results/processed/famcompare_analysis.json (scripts/analyze_famcompare.py);
%% figure scripts/plot_famcompare_ratio.py. Script-level self-checked, NOT
%% blind-/verify-gated (same evidentiary status as Fear x Structure below).
%% famcompare_analysis.json · 500 trials/cell · n=10000 · 95% bootstrap CIs in
%% famcompare_analysis.json · ER's open points are lower bounds
Fear multiplies the probability of ignition by roughly $3\times$ on the
heavy-tailed families (power-law and GIRG alike), and on the power-law model
that multiplier is \textbf{flat in $n$} at fixed $\bar\mu$: fear changes the
\emph{level} of ignition, not its \textbf{scaling}.

\vspace{0.3em}
How much fear amplifies is set by the structure it acts on. On ER at a
like-for-like baseline (seed chosen so zero-fear ignition sits at the same
few-percent level as the heavy-tailed families'), the same fear multiplies
ignition \textbf{at least $25$--$40\times$} --- most cells saturate at
certainty, so these are lower bounds. And with a bounded seed ($a = r = 2$),
ER \emph{never} ignites at any $\bar\mu$: fear amplifies a cascade the
structure makes possible; it cannot start one on its own.

\vspace{0.3em}
The multiplier is \emph{not} constant across $\bar\mu$ --- it falls to about
$1.5\times$ at $\bar\mu = 0.1$. Fear is an amplifier with a dial, not a
constant.

networks' by only $\sim$35\% at the same $\bar\mu = 0.4$ --- hubs blunt
fear's effect on the threshold, and geometry doesn't change that (CM and

\section{Where To Intervene}
%% Q9 panel — Gary's condensed wording, approved 2026-08-03 (S-063 session).
%% Mechanism rests on the AUDIT-PASS local-vs-global dichotomy (near-front fear
%% failures are redundant; remote pairs nucleate new fronts at r=2). The
%% quantitative nucleation law remains an honest negative (C-Q5(i)) — keep this
%% panel qualitative.
In a network, fear near an active failure is harmless, because those nearby
nodes would likely fail anyway. The real danger is fear reaching distant nodes
and starting new chain reactions --- so intervention should prioritize regions
far from the crisis, not just the active crisis zone.
