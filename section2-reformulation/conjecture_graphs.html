C1 — Asymptotic Decoupling ↑ §4.1

Conjecture: As $n \to \infty$, the generation sizes $(a_k)$ concentrate tightly around their deterministic mean-field trajectory, making the global fear fields $g_j$ asymptotically deterministic. Simulation confirms phase-boundary invariance within $\pm 0.03$.

The phase-diagram overlays below compare the empirical $P(\text{systemic})$ surface under the fear model at varying $\mu$ against the pure bootstrap-percolation ($\mu = 0$) boundary. Smooth, predictable deformation of the phase boundary with $\mu$ is the fingerprint of an asymptotically deterministic fear field — which is exactly what the decoupling conjecture predicts.

Fig C1a — Phase diagram, r = 2 $P(\text{systemic})$ heat-map with the $\mu = 0$ phase boundary overlaid. The boundary shift with $\mu$ is smooth and consistent with the mean-field saddle-node prediction.
Fig C1b — Phase diagram, r = 3 Same layout for threshold $r = 3$. Boundary invariance within $\pm 0.03$ holds across the parameter sweep.
Fig C1c — Phase diagram, r = 4 Same layout for threshold $r = 4$. Higher threshold narrows the transition window, sharpening the boundary.

C2 — Critical Seed Scaling Law ↑ §4.4

Conjecture: $a_c(\mu) = (1-\mu)^{r/(r-1)} \, a_c(0)$. Validated numerically at $n \in \{1000, 2000, 4000\}$; empirical ratios $a_c(\mu)/a_c(0)$ match the $(1-\mu)^{r/(r-1)}$ trend.

The scaling validation plots show the empirical critical seed size $a_c(\mu)$ — the threshold seed at which $P(\text{systemic}) = 0.5$ — as a function of $\mu$, normalized by $a_c(0)$, for $n \in \{1000, 2000, 4000\}$. Each panel overlays the mean-field prediction $(1-\mu)^{r/(r-1)}$. The exponent values are $r/(r-1) = 2$ for $r=2$, $\tfrac{3}{2}$ for $r=3$, and $\tfrac{4}{3}$ for $r=4$.

Fig C2a — Scaling validation, r = 2 Empirical $a_c(\mu)/a_c(0)$ vs $\mu$ for three system sizes, overlaid with $(1-\mu)^{2}$.
Fig C2b — Scaling validation, r = 3 Prediction curve $(1-\mu)^{3/2}$. Finite-$n$ curves converge toward the mean-field line as $n$ grows.
Fig C2c — Scaling validation, r = 4 Prediction curve $(1-\mu)^{4/3}$. Agreement is tightest at large $n$ and small $\mu$.
