## On the model as a Markov process

- The Markov process I'm studying — what you're studying is the stopping time of Markov
  chains. You are running some kind of Markov chain, and you stop when some condition is
  hit: when a certain number of nodes have failed, or any condition.
- The problem is usually that the stopping time is exponential in the size of the network,
  which is hard to simulate — a network of 10 million nodes (10⁶–10⁷) is hard.
- It makes sense why I didn't implement the healing.

## On geometry

- If you spread points uniformly at random, you'll see few points at the boundary.
- Basically, you can work with a network which has the hard RGG topology, with a tiny
  number of nodes that are affected.
- GIRG: a flexible model — the most general model.

## The poster

- I have enough material to make a good poster.
- What you want to do now is to have comparisons with respect to small conceptual changes:
  when you change the model a bit conceptually, you want to study the effect of geometry.
  You study Erdős–Rényi and GIRG, and set the average degree in both models to be the same.
  Then have a plot showing the effect of geometry.
- Another thing that is nice to add: you have one plot for the Erdős–Rényi graph, and you
  have plots depending on dimension. First, for hard RGG, keep increasing D and see whether
  you get different results or not.
- When you are doing GIRG, also set the parameters such that you can make a comparison with
  a configuration model.
- You need to do a comparison between the configuration model and the Erdős–Rényi graph.
  This is the effect of degree heterogeneity.
- Models with identical average degree and n — the degree distributions have identical
  mean — and plot the number of infected, or the probability of infection, in the
  configuration model vs. the Erdős–Rényi graph.
- Talk about inhomogeneous random graphs compared to configuration models. There you can
  also set the parameters such that the degree distribution is the same; one has geometry,
  the other has no geometry. The effect of geometry on the infection.
- If you make these two comparisons, then you can make a nice poster — a concrete enough
  introduction for this problem.
- The goal of the project is to understand the effect of degree heterogeneity, and also the
  geometry, as there is fear and whatnot localized, which is driven by localized events.
- Keep these comparisons separate:
  - the effect of heterogeneity;
  - the effect of geometry.
- When presenting this plot, cite Janson's paper as the reason Erdős–Rényi is the baseline,
  and then you start building on it. The baseline is Erdős–Rényi because of Janson's work.
  "Here is the framework; here is what I build on it."
