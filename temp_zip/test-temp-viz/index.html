A Two-Channel Cascade of Bank Failure

Bootstrap percolation (solvency) coupled with a self-referential global panic field (fear), on an Erdős–Rényi graph G(n, p).

1 · The model: two ways a bank can fail

The interbank network is an Erdős–Rényi random graph \(G(n,p)\). Each bank is either solvent or failed, and failure is absorbing — once failed, always failed. A bank can fail through either of two channels in a given round:

Channel 1 — Solvency (local, Janson)

A solvent bank fails once at least \(r\) of its neighbors have already failed:

\[ \bigl|\{\, j \in N(i) : j \text{ failed} \,\}\bigr| \ge r \]

This is the classic bootstrap-percolation rule. It is super-linear near zero for \(r \ge 2\): a tiny shock does nothing until enough neighbors pile up — which is why a macroscopic seed is needed to ignite a cascade.

Channel 2 — Fear (global, self-referential)

A global panic field equals the fraction of banks that failed in the previous round:

\[ g_t = \frac{a_{t-1}}{n} \]

A still-solvent bank \(i\) then fails with probability \(f_i \cdot g_t\), where \(f_i\) is its personal fear sensitivity.

Key fact. Fear is a subcritical amplifier: its per-round reproduction number is \(R_{\text{fear}} \approx \mu \lt 1\). It cannot ignite a cascade on its own — it only amplifies one the solvency channel has already started. Watch \(g_t\) in the sandbox spike then decay: that decay is this fact made visible.

Imagine a group of interconnected banks. When banks do business with each other, they form a web of relationships. If one bank collapses, it can drag others down. We model this using a simple network where banks are connected. A bank can fail in two different ways:

Channel 1 — The Domino Effect (Business Connections)

Banks lend to and borrow from their direct neighbors. If a bank's business partners fail, it loses money.

A healthy bank is sturdy: it can absorb some losses. But if too many of its partners fail—specifically, a number called the sturdiness threshold (\(r\))—the bank collapses like a domino.

Because banks are interconnected, a large enough initial shock can set off a chain reaction of business-partner failures across the network.

Channel 2 — The Rumor Effect (Public Panic)

In addition to actual business links, banks are vulnerable to panic. When depositors and investors see other banks failing in the news, they get scared.

In each round, a panic level is calculated based on what fraction of the network failed in the previous round.

A healthy bank can fail simply because its depositors panic and run on the bank, even if its direct partners are healthy. The risk depends on the bank's individual anxiety level.

Key takeaway: Panic amplifies, but it doesn't start the fire. On its own, panic behaves like a wave of anxiety: it spreads but quickly dies down unless a real, physical chain reaction of business failures (the domino effect) keeps feeding it. In the Sandbox below, you can watch the panic level spike and then decay when the domino effect fails to take off.

2 · The variables

Every symbol used on this page, grouped by role.

Network & solvency channel

Symbol What it is
\(r\) Capital-buffer depth — a bank fails if at least \(r\) of its neighbors have already failed; \(r \in \{2,3,4\}\) (tested).

Fear channel

Symbol What it is
\(\mu\) Average individual fear across all banks; the main sweep axis, \(\mu \in [0,1]\).
\(\kappa\) How tightly clustered individual fears are around \(\mu\) (Beta concentration parameter).
\(f_i\) Each bank's personal fear sensitivity, drawn once at \(t=0\) from \(\mathrm{Beta}(\mu\kappa,\,(1-\mu)\kappa)\), so \(\mathbb{E}[f_i]=\mu\) exactly.
\(g_t\) Global fear field at round \(t\): the fraction of banks that newly failed in the previous round, \(g_t = a_{t-1}/n\).
\(f_i\,g_t\) Probability bank \(i\) fails through fear in round \(t\) — a Bernoulli draw with this probability.

State variables (change each round)

Symbol What it is
\(A(t)\) Cumulative number of failed banks after round \(t\).
\(a\) Initial shock — how many banks start failed (seed size, at \(t=0\)).
\(a_t\) New failures in round \(t\). Drives the next round's fear field via \(g_{t+1}=a_t/n\).
\(A^{*}\) Final failed count when the cascade stops.
\(A^{*}/n\) Final failed fraction — the key outcome variable.
\(S(t)\) The set of nodes that failed in generation \(t\) due to solvency.
\(F(t)\) The set of nodes that failed in generation \(t\) due to fear.
\(k(t)\) The generation that "step" \(t\) belongs to.
\(T_{k(t)}\) The step count at which the current generation ends.

Measurement

Symbol What it is
\(\theta\) Systemic-event threshold: a run counts as "systemic" if \(A^{*}/n \ge \theta\); working value \(\theta = 0.5\).
\(P(\text{systemic})\) Fraction of simulation runs that end as systemic events — the phase diagram's \(y\)-axis.

Analytical benchmarks (no-fear baseline, \(\mu = 0\))

Symbol What it is
\(t_c\) Intermediate scale used to compute \(a_c\): \(\; t_c = \bigl((r-1)!\,/\,np^{r}\bigr)^{1/(r-1)}\).
\(a_c\) Janson critical seed size at \(\mu=0\): \(\; a_c = \tfrac{r-1}{r}\,t_c\).
\(a_c(\mu)\) Conjectured scaling law: \(\; a_c(\mu) = (1-\mu)^{\,r/(r-1)}\,a_c(0)\). The threshold shifts left as \(\mu\) grows.

Key terms and concepts used in this simulation:

Concept What it means
Bank Sturdiness (\(r\)) How many failed business partners a bank can absorb before it collapses. (e.g., \(r=2\) means it collapses only when a 2nd direct neighbor fails).
Average Anxiety (\(\mu\)) The average sensitivity of banks to public panic. A higher value means banks are more easily scared into failing by bad news.
Anxiety Spread (\(\kappa\)) How similarly anxious the banks are. A high value means all banks behave similarly; a low value means some are highly panic-prone while others are almost immune.
Initial Shock (\(a\)) The number of banks that are forced to fail at the very start due to an external economic shock.
Panic Level (\(g_t\)) The fraction of banks that collapsed in the previous round. This generates the "bad news" driving the panic effect.
Total Meltdown Threshold (\(\theta\)) The fraction of the network that must collapse (set to 50%) for the run to be classified as a "systemic meltdown."
