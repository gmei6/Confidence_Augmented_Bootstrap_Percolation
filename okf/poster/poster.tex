\section{The Setup}
\vspace{-0.3em}
The existing baseline (Janson) gives a bank exactly \textbf{one way to fail}: enough of its neighbors have already activated.

\vspace{0.3em}
Janson et al.\ solved that case on the Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi graph, as bootstrap percolation with threshold $r$. I add a \textbf{second way for nodes to activate}.

\vspace{-1.5em}
\section{The Model}
\vspace{-0.3em}
A node is activated if either condition is met:
\vspace{0.2em}
% Converted equations to inline math ($...$) to save ~2 inches of vertical space!
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=0.3em]
\item \textbf{Percolation:} At least $r$ of its neighbors have failed ($|\{j \sim i : j \in A_t\}| \geq r$).
\item \textbf{Fear:} A Bernoulli variable with probability $f_ig_t$ that a node activates ($U_i < f_i\, g_t$).
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{-1.5em}
\section{The Comparisons}
\vspace{-0.3em}
We hold network size $n$ and average degree constant across all tests to isolate three items:
\vspace{0.2em}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=0.3em]
  \item \textbf{Heterogeneity (ER vs.\ CM):} Homogeneous degree distribution vs.\ heavy-tailed hubs.
  \item \textbf{Geometry (CM vs.\ GIRG):} Pure random connections vs.\ local spatial clustering.
  \item \textbf{Fear ($\bar\mu = 0$ vs.\ $\bar\mu > 0$):} Baseline bootstrap percolation vs.\ confidence augmentation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Fear Amplifies}
%% ⚠ DO NOT DROP THE SECOND SENTENCE. Without it this reads as a flat "always
%% 3x", which is the exact claim S-058 caught and retracted on the advisor site:
%% the multiplier is ~3x at moderate mu-bar but only ~1.5x at mu-bar = 0.1. It is
%% constant IN n at fixed mu-bar -- not constant ACROSS mu-bar. See
%% okf/next-actions.md, "Multiplier claim qualified".
Fear multiplies the ignition probability by roughly $3\times$, and that multiplier
is \textbf{flat in $n$} at fixed $\bar\mu$; fear changes the \emph{level} of
ignition, not its \textbf{scaling}.

\vspace{0.3em}
The multiplier is \emph{not} constant across $\bar\mu$ --- it falls to about
$1.5\times$ at $\bar\mu = 0.1$. Fear is an amplifier with a dial, not a constant.

\section{Sharper Transition}
%% The lead-in was commented out, which left the two nu values printing with no
%% n-range and no statement of what nu is. A reader got two bare numbers.
Fear makes the systemic boundary \textbf{sharper}, leaving the system less
forgiving of small perturbations. Over $n = 10^3$ to $8\!\times\!10^4$ the
finite-size exponent $\nu$ --- the rate at which the transition window narrows
as the network grows --- contracts:
\begin{align*}
  \nu(\mu = 0)   &= 5.61 \pm 0.18 \\
  \nu(\mu = 0.3) &= 4.82 \pm 0.15
\end{align*}

\section{Where To Intervene}
%% ⚠ KEEP THIS LEAD-IN. Without it the section opens on "Calm the hubs" and reads
%% as a settled recommendation. It is not one -- Q9 is open (okf/open-questions),
%% and "calm the hubs" is the OBVIOUS lever, not a demonstrated optimal one.
How to allocate a fixed calming budget optimally is still an \textbf{open
question} --- it is what this work is now pointed at. The model does expose two
levers:
\vspace{0.3em}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, nosep, itemsep=0.3em]
  \item \textbf{Calm the hubs:} Tilting fear reduction toward high-degree nodes lowers the critical seed monotonically.
  \item \textbf{Limit visibility:} When the fear field is \textbf{local} rather than global, cascades behave like the zero-fear baseline. Panic requires a global audience.
\end{itemize}
