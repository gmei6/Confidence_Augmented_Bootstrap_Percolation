A Two-Channel Cascade of Bank Failure

Bootstrap percolation (solvency) coupled with a self-referential global panic field (fear), on an Erdős–Rényi graph G(n, p).

1 · The model: two ways a bank can fail

The interbank network is an Erdős–Rényi random graph \(G(n,p)\). Each bank is either solvent or failed, and failure is absorbing — once failed, always failed. A bank can fail through either of two channels in a given round:

Channel 1 — Solvency (local, Janson)

A solvent bank fails once at least \(r\) of its neighbors have already failed:

\[ \bigl|\{\, j \in N(i) : j \text{ failed} \,\}\bigr| \ge r \]

This is the classic bootstrap-percolation rule. It is super-linear near zero for \(r \ge 2\): a tiny shock does nothing until enough neighbors pile up — which is why a macroscopic seed is needed to ignite a cascade.

Channel 2 — Fear (global, self-referential)

A global panic field equals the fraction of banks that failed in the previous round:

\[ g_t = \frac{a_{t-1}}{n} \]

A still-solvent bank \(i\) then fails with probability \(f_i \cdot g_t\), where \(f_i\) is its personal fear sensitivity.

Key fact. Fear is a subcritical amplifier: its per-round reproduction number is \(R_{\text{fear}} \approx \mu < 1\). It cannot ignite a cascade on its own — it only amplifies one the solvency channel has already started. Watch \(g_t\) in the sandbox spike then decay: that decay is this fact made visible.

2 · The variables

Every symbol used on this page, grouped by role.

Network & solvency channel

SymbolWhat it is
\(r\)Capital-buffer depth — a bank fails if at least \(r\) of its neighbors have already failed; \(r \in \{2,3,4\}\) (tested).

Fear channel

SymbolWhat it is
\(\mu\)Average individual fear across all banks; the main sweep axis, \(\mu \in [0,1]\).
\(\kappa\)How tightly clustered individual fears are around \(\mu\) (Beta concentration parameter).
\(f_i\)Each bank's personal fear sensitivity, drawn once at \(t=0\) from \(\mathrm{Beta}(\mu\kappa,\,(1-\mu)\kappa)\), so \(\mathbb{E}[f_i]=\mu\) exactly.
\(g_t\)Global fear field at round \(t\): the fraction of banks that newly failed in the previous round, \(g_t = a_{t-1}/n\).
\(f_i\,g_t\)Probability bank \(i\) fails through fear in round \(t\) — a Bernoulli draw with this probability.

State variables (change each round)

SymbolWhat it is
\(A(t)\)Cumulative number of failed banks after round \(t\).
\(a\)Initial shock — how many banks start failed (seed size, at \(t=0\)).
\(a_t\)New failures in round \(t\). Drives the next round's fear field via \(g_{t+1}=a_t/n\).
\(A^{*}\)Final failed count when the cascade stops.
\(A^{*}/n\)Final failed fraction — the key outcome variable.
\(S(t)\)The set of nodes that failed in generation \(t\) due to solvency.
\(F(t)\)The set of nodes that failed in generation \(t\) due to fear.
\(k(t)\)The generation that "step" \(t\) belongs to.
\(T_{k(t)}\)The step count at which the current generation ends.

Measurement

SymbolWhat it is
\(\theta\)Systemic-event threshold: a run counts as "systemic" if \(A^{*}/n \ge \theta\); working value \(\theta = 0.5\).
\(P(\text{systemic})\)Fraction of simulation runs that end as systemic events — the phase diagram's \(y\)-axis.

Analytical benchmarks (no-fear baseline, \(\mu = 0\))

SymbolWhat it is
\(t_c\)Intermediate scale used to compute \(a_c\): \(\; t_c = \bigl((r-1)!\,/\,np^{r}\bigr)^{1/(r-1)}\).
\(a_c\)Janson critical seed size at \(\mu=0\): \(\; a_c = \tfrac{r-1}{r}\,t_c\).
\(a_c(\mu)\)Conjectured scaling law: \(\; a_c(\mu) = (1-\mu)^{\,r/(r-1)}\,a_c(0)\). The threshold shifts left as \(\mu\) grows.
