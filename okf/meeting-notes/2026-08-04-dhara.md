## Q&A

### Q: What were the configurations for each of the 3 graphs (poster hero comparison)?

All three families are matched on target mean degree and share the same fear/cascade
parameters — geometry/heterogeneity are the only things that differ between them. Configs:
`configs/poster_{er,cm,girg}_mu{0,40,70}.json`.

**Shared across all three** (`pinned_params` / `scaling`):
- $n = 10000$
- $r = 2$ (solvency rule: fails once 2 neighbors have failed)
- Fear Beta concentration $\kappa = 50$
- $\theta = 0.5$ (systemic threshold — cascade must reach 50% of $n$)
- Fear kernel: `window_len=5`, uniform weights $[0.2]\times5$
- `target_high_degree: false`
- Matched mean degree: `target_mean_degree = 4.5336` (Janson scaling, `alpha=0.6`, `n_ref=10000`)
- Hero-figure sweep: $\bar\mu \in \{0, 0.4, 0.7\}$, 500 trials/cell, `base_seed=42`

**Per-family (`graph` block):**

| Family | `graph.type` | Parameters | Engine |
|---|---|---|---|
| ER | *(omitted → defaults to `"gnp"`, i.e. $G(n,p)$)* | $p$ derived from the matched mean degree | **C++** |
| Configuration model (CM) | `"configuration_model"` | power-law degree sequence, $\tau=2.5$, $d_{\min}=2$ | Python (reference) |
| GIRG | `"girg"` | $\tau=2.5$, geometric kernel exponent $\alpha_g=1.2$, $w_{\min}=0.186377$ (calibrated via `scripts/calibrate_girg_degree.py` to hit the same $\langle k\rangle$) | Python (reference) |

Note the cross-engine split (ER on C++, CM/GIRG on Python) is disclosed explicitly in

## Poster design directives

### On what a poster is for

A poster's job is to grab attention and hold it. Nice pictures matter because they give the
presenter something to point at while talking — the listener looks where you point, then
the picture makes what you're saying click. The poster is a prop for the conversation, not
a standalone document.

### Add a visual model diagram

Missing from the current draft: a small schematic of the model itself. A small network
graph with:
- a few **red** nodes = infected/failed
- some nodes shown with **fear** (a distinct visual encoding — color/shading — from
  infection)
- colors used consistently to distinguish different node states

### Phrasing directive — motivate the fear+heterogeneity+geometry study explicitly

Use language like: *"there is a need, in addition to studying [the baseline], to model and
study the effect of fear on different [network] models with heterogeneity and geometry."*
State this as an explicit added-value/motivation sentence, not left implicit in the figure
comparisons.

### Suggested talk flow (baseline-first)

1. **Who used [the baseline model] and why** — establish why it's the baseline, why we use
   it at all.
2. **Then how it works.**

Suggested section format: **Our model → Goal → What are we looking for.**

### Suggested overall poster structure (linear)

- Title
- What I'm talking about
- What exists
- What I did

### Alternative phrasing of the same arc

- Introduce the problem
- Why it's interesting
- What's been done
- What we did

**Synthesis:** both structural suggestions describe the same narrative arc — problem/context
→ existing/baseline work → this project's contribution — just phrased twice with slightly
different labels. Poster reorganization should converge the two into one section flow rather
than treating them as two separate proposals.
