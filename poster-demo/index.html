Poster notes, Fear Amplifies

The y-axis of the figure is the fear multiplier: how many times more likely a system-wide cascade becomes when banks carry fear level μ̄ compared with the same network at zero fear (n = 10,000, 500 trials per point).

On the heavy-tailed families (power-law and GIRG), fear multiplies ignition by roughly 3×, and that multiplier stays flat as the network grows, fear raises the level of risk, not its scaling.

On an Erdős–Rényi network at a matched starting risk (a seed chosen so its zero-fear ignition rate matches the heavy-tailed families’ few percent), the same fear multiplies ignition at least 25–40×. The open points on the figure mean every one of 500 trials cascaded, the measurement hit its ceiling, so the true multiplier is higher still.

But with a small bounded seed (a = r = 2), ER never ignites at any fear level: fear amplifies cascades the structure makes possible; it cannot start them alone. And the multiplier has a dial, at low fear (μ̄ = 0.1) it is only about 1.5×.

About the animations

Every screen plays a precomputed trace. Each cascade was run once through src/twocascade/reference.py, the project's source-of-truth engine, at n = 300 with a logged seed, and dumped to JSON by scripts/dump_poster_demo_traces.py. The page only plays those files back; it never simulates anything, so what you see is deterministic and reproducible from a committed config and seed, exactly like the figures on the poster.

Each failure is labelled structural or fear after the fact, from the graph alone: a bank that already had r = 2 failed neighbours when it fell is structural, otherwise it panicked. At zero fear every single failure classifies structural, that is the check that keeps the labelling honest.

The three network families are drawn at a matched average degree (about 4.0 links per bank at n = 300), so the differences you see come from the shape of the network, not from one of them simply having more links.

Demo scale is not poster scale

These animations run at n = 300 so they fit on a phone; the poster's results are at n = 10,000 and above. Small networks flatter the underdog: a two-bank shock sits about 4.6× below the critical seed size at n = 300 but about 122× below it at n = 10,000, so rare things that never happen on the poster can happen here. Where that matters, the screen says so rather than generalising the small picture.
